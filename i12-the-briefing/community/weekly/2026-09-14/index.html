Better data for tools that read the rules

A community report identifies timeframes expressed in prose but missing structured fields. A September reply acknowledges the gaps and identifies the release intended to address them.

Why it matters & sources

Machine-readable metadata matters to teams building tools. Follow the report and the published changelog together.

Scope starts with what a system can affect

A page discussion uses contrasting examples to explain why scope depends on a system’s relationship to federal customer data, rather than its corporate label.

Why it matters & sources

Read the minimum-assessment-scope rules with the architecture in view. The reply illustrates the reasoning without creating a universal exemption.

  • Reply by @pete-gov ↗

Follow the replies, not just the pages

A reader asks how to discover comments embedded in the rules pages. The reply points to the underlying GitHub discussions and ways to follow activity in both repositories.

Why it matters & sources

This hub follows that same distinction: new comments on old threads are new activity, even when the opening post has not changed.

  • Reply by @pete-gov ↗

Agency questions meet provider evidence

A discussion about legacy artifacts leads to an explanation of the different roles of agency plans and provider security information.

Why it matters & sources

Keep the agency’s use decision distinct from the provider’s certification work. The exchange provides context; the linked agency-use material provides the requirements.

  • Reply by @pete-gov ↗

Two proposals invite public feedback

New requests for comment cover 20x Phase 4 development tracks for Class D and changes to the Technical Advisory Group. Each has a dedicated proposal and comment process.

Why it matters & sources

Use the formal RFC threads for feedback on the proposals. General discussion is useful context, but it is a different channel.

RFC 0032 · Offerings By Government

This RFC proposes a pathway for federal agency-operated cloud service offerings to obtain FedRAMP Certification under their own agency authorization process, exempting them from FedRAMP rules designed for private companies while preserving minimum government-wide information requirements to support reuse by customer agencies.
