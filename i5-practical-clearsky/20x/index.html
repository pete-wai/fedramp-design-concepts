FedRAMP 20x

Your Class A starting point.

A smaller first step still needs a complete package. Bring the work you have, identify what is missing, and give your team a concrete plan.

Check your foundation.

Complete an approved alternative framework process within the past 12 months, including independent assessment where applicable. Approved options include SOC 2 Type II, GovRAMP, or FedRAMP Rev5 (including FedRAMP Ready).

  • Gather the complete assessment and supporting materials, including any applicable bridge or gap letter.
  • Define the service boundary, intended agency use and missing Class A evidence.
  • Prepare the required package information and ongoing reporting arrangements.

Additional independent verification and validation of the Class A package is optional. The assessment required by your underlying framework still applies.

Read the complete Class A requirements

Show that you are preparing.

Actively preparing providers can request an Initial Implementation Phase listing. Obtain the listing before applying for certification. Prepare and submit your request this week as a team goal; FedRAMP controls publication timing. Listing does not mean certification.

Start with your team and AI support.

Use current official rules to navigate requirements, organize existing evidence and prepare drafts before committing to a large consulting engagement. Check every output against sources and your service. Your team remains accountable.

Choose the assurance your customers need.

Class A can be a beginning. B and C bring additional requirements and ongoing commitments. A→B→C is a possible journey, not a required sequence or automatic upgrade. Classes are not direct equivalents of agency impact levels.

Compare classes

Keep the agency decision in view.

Certification provides reusable evidence. Each agency assesses and authorizes its own use. Agencies SHOULD NOT authorize Class A use for more than 12 months unless the offering is actively seeking B, C or D; this is not a universal certification expiry.

Give engineering a practical brief.

Document your service boundary, assessment dates, evidence gaps, owners and intended agency use. Separate preparation, engineering changes, submission and review.

Planning targets for a prepared team

Aim for two engineers, two weeks, to a working package prototype; two months to certification; and $10,000–$20,000 in additional external spending on tools and help, excluding existing SOC 2 costs and internal engineering labor.

These are proposed targets, not measured averages, cost estimates or promises. Actual effort depends on readiness; certification timing also depends on FedRAMP review.
