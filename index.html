The ideas in the design.

A familiar front door
A consistent homepage may reduce the effort of finding a path on return visits.
Participation before the stream
Open House starts with ways to meet, read and contribute, then explains what changed.
Synthesis with a source trail
Weekly groupings aim to make a busy week understandable while preserving access to original sources and comments.
Different audiences, different starting points
Provider tasks and agency questions shape the new sections. Details unfold after orientation.

These are design hypotheses. We have not demonstrated improved comprehension or task success with participants.

What we should learn next.

  1. Validate the paths. Can providers and agency readers recognize their starting point and explain where it leads?
  2. Choose the first real resources. Select an engineering walkthrough and an agency planning worksheet; then source and review their content.
  3. Define the editorial practice. Agree who owns the Monday summary, source review, corrections and freshness.
  4. Test the complete experience. Observe navigation, comprehension and accessibility with intended users before committing to implementation.

What is real here?

The navigation, disclosures, themes and local interaction demos work. The new resource content is explicitly placeholder material. Community items remain the October 4, 2026 snapshot; Monday editions are historical examples. Homepage metrics are illustrative. Nothing is submitted, assessed or approved.

Iteration 12 stays available for comparison. Earlier iterations are removed from the public collection and retained in Git history and local recovery snapshots.

Validation: build and browser review results are recorded in the repository’s contributor guide. Checks do not constitute production readiness or an accessibility certification.
